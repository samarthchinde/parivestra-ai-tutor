# Class 3 and 4 English Knowledge Base

## Chunk: eng_c3_nouns
Title: Nouns and Naming Words
Subject: English
Topic: Nouns
Grade: Class 3
Keywords: noun, naming word, person, place, animal, thing, proper noun, common noun
Content:
A noun is a naming word. It names a person (like teacher or boy), a place (like school or park), an animal (like dog or elephant), or a thing (like pencil or book). Common nouns name general items (like city or girl). Proper nouns name specific people, special days, or exact places (like Pari, Delhi, or Sunday), and they always begin with a capital letter.

## Chunk: eng_c3_verbs
Title: Action Verbs and Doing Words
Subject: English
Topic: Verbs
Grade: Class 3
Keywords: verb, action words, doing words, run, jump, sing, read, play
Content:
A verb is an action word that tells what someone or something does. Words like hop, skip, run, read, smile, and write are all verbs. For example, in the sentence "Pari reads an exciting book," the word "reads" is the action verb. Every complete English sentence must have at least one verb to tell the action happening.

## Chunk: eng_c4_adjectives
Title: Adjectives and Describing Words
Subject: English
Topic: Adjectives
Grade: Class 4
Keywords: adjective, describing words, color, size, shape, feeling, gentle, bright
Content:
An adjective is a word that describes or tells more about a noun. Adjectives can describe color (a blue sky), size (a tiny ant, an enormous tree), shape (a round ball), or feeling (a happy student). In the phrase "three sweet yellow bananas," the words "three", "sweet", and "yellow" are all adjectives describing the bananas.

## Chunk: eng_c4_sentences
Title: Sentences and Punctuation
Subject: English
Topic: Sentences
Grade: Class 4
Keywords: sentence, punctuation, capital letter, full stop, question mark, exclamation mark
Content:
A sentence is a group of words that makes complete sense together. A sentence always begins with a capital letter. If it tells a fact, it ends with a full stop (period). If it asks something, it ends with a question mark (?). If it shows strong excitement or surprise, it ends with an exclamation mark (!). For example: "Where is the red ball?" ends with a question mark.
